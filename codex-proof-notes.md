## Hartshorne III.2.7 proof notes

Source: `Aristotle/GrothendieckVanishing/context/Hartshorne_3_2.pdf`

These are working notes extracted from the scanned PDF so the formalization can proceed without
repeated image inspection.

### Proposition 2.9

Let `X` be a noetherian topological space and let `(F_α)` be a directed system of abelian sheaves.
Then for each `i ≥ 0` there are natural isomorphisms

`colim_α H^i(X, F_α) ≅ H^i(X, colim_α F_α)`.

Hartshorne’s proof:

- For each `x ∈ X` there is a natural map `colim_α F_{α, x} → (colim_α F_α)_x`.
- This induces a map on cohomology and then a map of delta-functors
  `colim_α H^i(X, F_α) → H^i(X, colim_α F_α)`.
- They agree for `i = 0`, so to prove they are isomorphisms it is enough to show both sides are
  effaceable for `i > 0`.
- For each `α`, let `G_α` be the sheaf of discontinuous sections of `F_α` (Hartshorne II, Ex. 1.16e).
- Each `G_α` is flasque, and there is a natural inclusion `F_α → G_α`.
- The construction is functorial, so the `G_α` form a directed system, and
  `colim_α G_α` is flasque by Lemma 2.8.
- Therefore `H^i(X, G_α) = 0` for `i > 0`, and also `H^i(X, colim_α G_α) = 0` for `i > 0`.
- So both delta-functors are effaceable in positive degrees, hence isomorphic.

### Lemma 2.10

Let `Y` be a closed subset of `X`, let `F` be a sheaf of abelian groups on `Y`, and let
`j : Y → X` be the inclusion. Then

`H^i(Y, F) = H^i(X, j_* F)`,

where `j_* F` is the extension/direct image of `F` by zero outside `Y`
(Hartshorne II, Ex. 1.19).

### Theorem III.2.7

Goal: if `X` is noetherian and `n > dim X`, then `H^n(X, F) = 0` for every sheaf `F`.

Hartshorne proves this by induction on `n = dim X`, with several substeps.

#### Step 1: reduction to irreducible `X`

- Assume `X` is reducible.
- Let `Y` be one irreducible component and let `U = X \ Y`.
- For any sheaf `F` there is an exact sequence

  `0 → F_U → F → F_Y → 0`.

- From the long exact sequence of cohomology, it is enough to prove
  `H^i(X, F_Y) = 0` and `H^i(X, F_U) = 0` for `i > n`.
- `F_Y` can be regarded as a sheaf on the closed subset `Y`.
- `F_U` can be regarded as a sheaf on the closed subset `Ū`.
- `Y` and `Ū` have fewer irreducible components than `X`.
- Using Lemma 2.10 and induction on the number of irreducible components, reduce to irreducible
  `X`.

#### Step 2: irreducible space of dimension `0`

- Assume `X` is irreducible and `dim X = 0`.
- Then the only open subsets are `∅` and `X`.
- Therefore every sheaf is flasque.
- Hence `H^i(X, F) = 0` for `i > 0` by Proposition 2.5.

#### Step 3: reduction to a sheaf generated by one section

- Assume `X` is irreducible of dimension `n`, and let `F ≠ 0`.
- Let `B = ⋃_{x ∈ X} F_x`, and let `A` be the set of finite subsets of `B`.
- For each `α ∈ A`, let `F_α` be the subsheaf of `F` generated by the sections in `α`
  (over suitable open sets).
- Then `A` is directed and `F = colim_α F_α`.
- By Proposition 2.9 it is enough to prove vanishing for each `F_α`.
- If `α' ⊆ α`, there is an exact sequence

  `0 → F_{α'} → F_α → G → 0`,

  where `G` is generated by `#(α \ α')` sections.
- Using the long exact sequence and induction on `#α`, reduce to the case where `F`
  is generated by a single section over some open set `U`.
- In that case `F` is a quotient of `Z_U`, where `Z` denotes the constant sheaf `ℤ` on `X`.
- Let `R` be the kernel. Then there is an exact sequence

  `0 → R → Z_U → F → 0`.

- Again by the long exact sequence, it is enough to prove vanishing for `R` and `Z_U`.

#### Step 4: reduction from a subsheaf of `Z_U` to a smaller closed set

- Let `U` be open and let `R` be a subsheaf of `Z_U`.
- For each `x ∈ U`, the stalk `R_x` is a subgroup of `ℤ`.
- If `R = 0`, skip to Step 5.
- Otherwise, let `d` be the least positive integer that occurs in some stalk `R_x`.
- Then there exists a nonempty open subset `V ⊆ U` such that `R|_V ≅ d·ℤ_V` as a subsheaf of
  `ℤ|_V`.
- Hence `R|_V = ℤ_V`, so there is an exact sequence

  `0 → Z_V → R → R/Z_V → 0`.

- The sheaf `R/Z_V` is supported on the closed subset `(U \ V)¯` of `X`.
- Since `X` is irreducible, `(U \ V)¯` has dimension `< n`.
- By Lemma 2.10 and the induction hypothesis on dimension,
  `H^i(X, R/Z_V) = 0` for `i ≥ n`.
- Therefore it remains only to show vanishing for `Z_V`.

#### Step 5: vanishing for `Z_U`

- It is enough to show that for every open subset `U ⊆ X`, one has `H^i(X, Z_U) = 0` for `i > n`.
- Let `Y = X \ U`.
- There is an exact sequence

  `0 → Z_U → Z → Z_Y → 0`.

- Since `X` is irreducible, `dim Y < dim X = n`.
- By Lemma 2.10 and the induction hypothesis, `H^i(X, Z_Y) = 0` for `i ≥ n`.
- On the other hand, `Z` is flasque because it is a constant sheaf on an irreducible space.
- Hence `H^i(X, Z) = 0` for `i > 0`.
- The long exact sequence gives `H^i(X, Z_U) = 0` for `i > n`.

### Formalization pressure points

These are the places the Lean development still has to build:

- Lemma 2.10: cohomology on a closed subspace equals cohomology of the pushforward to `X`.

- Step 3 construction of finitely generated subsheaves and their directed colimit.
- Step 4 support-on-closed-subset reduction.
- Step 5 exact sequence `0 → Z_U → Z → Z_Y → 0`.

## Codex notes

### Closed inclusion route

For a closed subset `s ⊆ X` with inclusion `i : s → X`, the natural counit

`i^* i_* F → F`

should be an isomorphism. In Lean this is

`(TopCat.Sheaf.pullbackPushforwardAdjunction AddCommGrpCat (closedIncl hs)).counit.app F`.

The workable formal route is:

- reduce isomorphism of sheaves to isomorphism on stalks via
  `TopCat.Presheaf.isIso_of_stalkFunctor_map_iso`
- identify the stalk map as a composition of four maps:
  1. the stalk map of `TopCat.Sheaf.pullbackIso`
  2. the inverse stalk map of `CategoryTheory.toSheafify`
  3. `TopCat.Presheaf.stalkPullbackIso.inv`
  4. `TopCat.Presheaf.stalkPushforward`
- prove the stalk map of `toSheafify` is an iso by bijectivity:
  injective on stalks from local injectivity, surjective on stalks from local surjectivity

The remaining Lean issue is a single explicit stalk-level identity between the counit germ
and the above composition. This is the key missing technical lemma for Hartshorne Lemma 2.10.

### Current main-file route

The current `Setup.lean` no longer phrases the remaining work as a direct `j_!` construction.
Instead it isolates two closed-immersion statements:

- `PushforwardHVanishing`:
  if `G` on a closed subset `Z` has `H^n(G) = 0`, then `H^n(i_* G) = 0` on `X`.
- `closedImmersionSES`:
  for a closed subset `Z ⊆ X` and sheaf `F` on `X`, there is a short exact sequence
  `0 → K → F → i_* i^* F → 0`.

If these two are proved, then:

- `ReducibleVanishing` follows by peeling off irreducible components and applying the LES.
- `IrreduciblePosVanishing` follows by iteratively enlarging the closed subset on which the kernel
  has zero stalks, using noetherianity to terminate.

So, even if Hartshorne’s paper motivates the use of `j_!`, the immediate formal blocker in the
current branch is still the closed-subspace comparison, not the top-level induction shell.

### Zero-outside scratch route

In `codex-file.lean` there is now a local `zeroOutside` development:

- `TopCat.Presheaf.zeroOutside`
- `TopCat.Sheaf.zeroOutsideInt`
- `TopCat.Sheaf.epi_map_of_injective`

This is the `j_! ℤ_U` fragment needed for the “generated by one section” part of Hartshorne’s
irreducible argument. It is not yet wired into the main files, but it is useful scratch
infrastructure if the closed-immersion route needs to be supplemented by an explicit open-side
construction.

### Alternative route checked and not sufficient on its own

Mathlib already provides contravariant Ext long-exact-sequence tools via
`CategoryTheory/Abelian/Projective/Dimension.lean`, and also the Mayer-Vietoris short exact
sequence for free abelian sheaves on opens via
`CategoryTheory.Sites.MayerVietorisSquare.shortComplex`.

This suggests trying to prove projective-dimension bounds for the sheaves `Z_U`.

What works:

- Mayer-Vietoris gives short exact sequences
  `0 → Z_{U ∩ V} → Z_U ⊕ Z_V → Z_{U ∪ V} → 0`
- projective-dimension lemmas
  `hasProjectiveDimensionLT_X₁/X₂/X₃`
  can transport bounds across such short exact sequences

Why this does not yet replace Hartshorne’s closed-subset route:

- for reducible `X`, the natural opens attached to irreducible components do not cover `X`
- for irreducible `X`, nonempty opens have dense closure, so Mayer-Vietoris alone does not
  give the strict dimension drop needed in Step 5

So the Mayer-Vietoris infrastructure is useful, but by itself it does not eliminate the need
for the closed-subspace comparison / support-on-closed-subset machinery.
